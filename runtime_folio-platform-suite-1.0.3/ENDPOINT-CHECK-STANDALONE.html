Every declared route

One URL can carry many routes: the flow switches on the discriminator in the body. 39 routes across 19 endpoints — SUBSIDIARY_ACTIONS alone carries eighteen.

A flow that answers on its first route and has no case for the rest passes the Endpoints tab and fails at an officer's desk. This sends each route separately.

Read this before trusting a green row

A route probe proves the flow accepted a request carrying that discriminator. It cannot prove the flow implements it: a flow whose switch has a permissive default answers 200 to a route it does nothing with. Treat a green row as "not refused", and confirm behaviour by looking at what the flow actually did.

The discriminator is sent as both action and name, because the documented estate uses both conventions and no single one is right for every flow.

Reading a result

      
answered — the flow replied. If it replied without
        the keys the platform reads, that is named, and it is the finding worth having: the
        flow is live but its response does not carry what the client needs.

      
refused — a 4xx the flow itself produced. The flow is
        live and validating its input. For UPLOAD and STATUS a
        refusal is the correct answer and is marked as a pass.

      
signature — 401 or 403 from Power Automate. The
        trigger URL was regenerated, or it never authenticated. Rotate and rebuild.

      
no flow — 404 on the trigger path. The flow was
        deleted or the URL is stale.

      
not reached — the call never got an answer from
        Power Automate. A browser CORS rejection, an offline network, or something in the
        middle answering instead. This says nothing about the endpoint. A
        CORS rejection gives this page no status and no body, so it cannot be told apart from
        the host being unreachable — and neither is evidence that the signature is
        wrong.
